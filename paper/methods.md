The file holds 130,912 records <!-- results/filter_log.csv: step 1, count_before -->. All lengths are measured in the layer's own coordinate system, EPSG:26915 <!-- results/filter_log.csv: step 4, description --> (NAD83 / UTM zone 15N, in meters <!-- CRS name: CLAUDE.md:8, not in results/ -->).

## Size measures

Area is the DNR acres attribute of each record, not the area of the polygon. Shoreline length (shoreline_m) is the perimeter of the polygon in EPSG:26915, summed over every ring: the outer shore and the shore of each island.

## Box-counting dimension

The boundary of a lake is every ring of its polygon, the outer shore and every island shore: the same boundary that shoreline length measures. The boundary is densified so that consecutive points are no more than one-tenth of the smallest box size apart <!-- results/validation/spacing_0p01/parameters.csv: planned_densify_fraction; set in src/boxcount.py:32 (DENSIFY_FRACTION = 0.1) -->. For a box size s, a square grid with cells of side s is laid over the lake, and N(s) is the number of cells that hold at least one boundary point. The dimension is the least-squares slope of log N(s) against log(1/s) over the box sizes of a window.

Box counts depend on where the grid starts, so the grid origin is shifted in x and y by a random fraction of s. Eight such offsets are drawn with seed 0 <!-- results/primary/parameters.csv: grid_offsets, grid_seed -->. One slope is fitted per offset, and the lake's dimension is the median slope; its r² is the median r² of the per-offset fits. The same offset fractions are used for every lake.

**Windows.** Each window has 8 box sizes, evenly spaced on a log scale <!-- results/primary/parameters.csv: box_sizes_per_window -->, and all windows are run on the same lakes.

- Primary window: 20 to 100 m <!-- results/primary/rho_primary.csv: window 20_100, window_min_m and window_max_m -->.
- Sensitivity windows: 10 to 100 m and 20 to 200 m <!-- results/primary/rho_primary.csv: windows 10_100 and 20_200, window_min_m and window_max_m -->.

**Fit quality.** For each window, lakes with r² below 0.98 <!-- results/primary/parameters.csv: r2_threshold --> are counted and reported. No lake is excluded for poor fit.

**Checks on known shapes.** The estimator is tested on shapes of known dimension (tests/test_boxcount.py): a circle (1), a Koch snowflake (log 4 / log 3, about 1.262) and a Sierpinski triangle (log 3 / log 2, about 1.585) <!-- results/validation/benchmarks.csv: known_d; also CLAUDE.md:68, not in results/ -->, with box sizes from 10 to 100 m <!-- src/boxcount.py:16 (DEFAULT_BOX_SIZES), not in results/ -->. A test passes if the estimate is within 0.05 of the known value <!-- tests/test_boxcount.py:28 (TOLERANCE), not in results/ -->. This tolerance guards against changes to the code; it is not a measure of accuracy, which the post-hoc calibration below measures. A first Koch test with a smaller snowflake failed and was replaced by a larger snowflake that is self-similar over the whole box range; the test's docstring records the failure. The snowflake that replaced it, which was the test in place when the primary results were computed, was built wrongly: its bumps pointed inward, so the polygon crossed itself and its area was about 2/5 of the starting triangle instead of 8/5 <!-- geometric limits, recorded in the docstring of test_koch_snowflake_dimension_is_about_1_262 in tests/test_boxcount.py; not in results/ -->. The test passed anyway, because box counting uses only the boundary points. This was found after review and corrected in commit f9e2f28. The bumps now point outward, every test shape must be a valid polygon, and the snowflake's area is checked against 8/5 of the triangle <!-- tests/test_boxcount.py, test_koch_snowflake_area_is_8_5_of_the_starting_triangle; not in results/ -->. The corrected snowflake has 7 iterations on a 7,290 m side, so its smallest segment, 3.3 m <!-- results/validation/benchmarks.csv: parameters, iterations=7; side_m=7290; smallest_segment_m=3.33 -->, is at most one-third of the smallest box in any window, and the side is more than ten times the largest box <!-- sizing rule: docstring of test_koch_snowflake_dimension_is_about_1_262 in tests/test_boxcount.py, not in results/ -->. The error was in the test shape, not in the box counting. The box-counting code gained only an optional point-spacing argument, and at the planned spacing its output is checked against that of the version that computed the primary results: the box counts exactly, and the floating values within a relative and absolute tolerance of 1e-12 <!-- tests/conftest.py (RTOL, ATOL), not in results/ --> (tests/test_boxcount.py, test_default_output_matches_3b85cc7, since commit 03e8013). On the original computer the output is also identical bit for bit, a check run on request (test_default_output_is_byte_identical_to_3b85cc7, with LAKE_FRACTALS_EXACT=1). On another system, a reviewer's run of the tests gave floating values that agreed to about 1e-15, with identical box counts <!-- reported by the review, not in results/ -->.

**Post-hoc validation of the estimator (not in the analysis plan).** After review and after the primary results, the estimator was checked further (src/validation.py, commit f9e2f28; results/validation/). Before this, only the circle had been tested in all three windows (tests/test_primary_analysis.py). The circle (radius 2,000 m), the Koch snowflake at 6 and 7 iterations (side 7,290 m) and the Sierpinski triangle (8 levels, side 1,280 m) were box-counted in all three windows at the planned point spacing <!-- results/validation/benchmarks.csv: shape, parameters, window -->. For a calibration near 1, shapes were built whose dimension is known and in the range of the lake estimates. A generalized Koch snowflake, in which each segment's bump makes an angle θ with the segment, has dimension log 4 / log(2 + 2 cos θ) <!-- formula: src/validation.py:86-93 (koch_cos_theta, koch_dimension), not in results/ -->; three were built, of dimension 1.02, 1.05 and 1.10, with 6 iterations on a 7,290 m side <!-- results/validation/calibration.csv: known_d, cos_theta, iterations, side_m -->. As smooth shapes the size of lakes, with dimension 1, ellipses were built of 100, 1,000 and 10,000 acres with axis ratios of 1, 3 and 10 <!-- results/validation/calibration.csv: target_acres, aspect_ratio -->, with vertices at most 10 m apart along the edge <!-- src/validation.py:65 (ELLIPSE_VERTEX_SPACING_M), not in results/ -->. Each ellipse was drawn 40 times <!-- results/validation/calibration_summary.csv: n --> with a random rotation and shift, from seed 0 <!-- src/validation.py:73 (ELLIPSE_SEED), not in results/ -->; the same draws were used for every area and axis ratio. The ranges, rotation from 0 to 180° and shift from 0 to 200 m in x and y, were not set by the author <!-- src/validation.py:69-74, not in results/ -->. The box-counting grid is placed relative to the lower-left corner of the boundary points, so a shift leaves the box counts unchanged up to floating-point rounding; only the rotation varies between draws. The calibration shapes and the benchmark circle were run in all three windows with boundary points at most 0.1 (planned), 0.03 and 0.01 of the smallest box apart <!-- results/validation/calibration.csv: densify_fraction -->. Every benchmark and calibration shape is a valid polygon <!-- results/validation/benchmarks.csv: is_valid; results/validation/calibration_summary.csv: all_valid -->.

### Why the primary sample starts at 100 acres <!-- results/primary/parameters.csv: primary_min_acres -->

The primary sample was moved from the 10-acre lakes to the 100-acre lakes <!-- 10: results/filter_log.csv, step 2 description; 100: results/primary/parameters.csv, primary_min_acres -->, which had been the robustness check, for two reasons. The move was made after the pilot and before the full run, in the analysis plan (commit c4abc80). The two reasons were first written down after the primary results, in commit e8cdbec.

**Finite-size bias.** A 10-acre lake is about 230 m across, roughly twice the 100 m box and close to the 200 m box <!-- author's figures, not in results/: 230 m is the equivalent diameter, 2 * sqrt(area / pi), of a 10-acre circle, and "twice" compares it with the 100 m box. 10: results/filter_log.csv, step 2 description; 100 m and 200 m: results/primary/rho_primary.csv, window_max_m -->. When the largest box approaches the width of the lake, the box count levels off. This biases the dimension in one direction and can create a size trend on its own. In the primary sample the smallest lake has an equivalent diameter of 718 m <!-- results/feasibility/feasibility_summary.txt: min 717.8 (not a csv) -->, so the largest primary box is under one-seventh of the smallest lake's width <!-- author's figure, from the 100 m box and 717.8 m; not in results/ -->.

**Timing.** The switch was made after the 200-lake pilot <!-- results/pilot/pilot_dims.csv: one row per pilot lake --> (commit d5a7e1d), which showed that the size–dimension relationship changed with the window and was strongest in small lakes. The choice was therefore informed by the data, not specified in advance.

**Effect on the area result.** The 10-acre exploratory result <!-- results/primary/exploratory_10acre_r2_below_threshold.csv: min_acres --> shows that this choice changes the area conclusion. In the 10-acre sample, Spearman rho between dimension and area in the primary window is -0.2831 (95% CI -0.2988 to -0.2676) <!-- results/primary/exploratory_10acre_rho.csv: area -->, against -0.0048 (95% CI -0.0388 to 0.0291) in the primary sample <!-- results/primary/rho_primary.csv: 20_100, area -->. The 10-acre sample <!-- results/primary/exploratory_10acre_r2_below_threshold.csv: min_acres --> was run in the primary window only, so the decision rule was not applied to it.

### Exploratory sample

The 10-acre analysis is exploratory, and every 10-acre result is labeled exploratory <!-- results/primary/exploratory_10acre_r2_below_threshold.csv: min_acres -->. It was run in the primary window only. That window was chosen for this run (src/exploratory_10acre.py) and was not specified in the protocol.

## Statistical analysis

**Correlations.** In the primary sample, Spearman rho between dimension and each size measure, area and shoreline length, is computed in each window.

**Confidence intervals.** Each rho has a 95% <!-- results/primary/parameters.csv: ci_level_percent --> percentile bootstrap interval. Lakes are resampled with replacement 2,000 times with seed 0 <!-- results/primary/parameters.csv: bootstrap_resamples, bootstrap_seed -->, and the interval spans the central 95% <!-- results/primary/parameters.csv: ci_level_percent --> of the resampled rhos. The generator is re-seeded for each rho, so every rho within a sample is computed on the same resampled sets of lakes. The protocol fixed the number of resamples and the seed but not the interval method. The percentile method was recorded in an addendum written after the primary results (commit ff3908c); BCa intervals were considered after the results were reviewed and were not used.

**Decision rule (planned).** The rule is applied separately to two relationships: dimension vs area, and dimension vs shoreline length. A relationship is supported only if rho has the same sign in all three windows <!-- results/primary/decision_rule.csv: columns rho_20_100, rho_10_100, rho_20_200 -->; otherwise it is reported as window-dependent. p-values and confidence intervals are reported but are not part of the rule.

**Mapping source comparison.** The delineat_1 attribute records how each shoreline was drawn. Source groups were set on the 10-acre sample <!-- results/filter_log.csv: step 2 description -->, counted before the shoreline and outside_mn steps. Each value with at least 1,000 lakes stands alone: ORIG DLG (4,094), 2004 FSA (3,378), DNR 24k POLYS (3,182) and PWI (1,461) <!-- CLAUDE.md:46, not in results/ -->. Every other value, including 2010 FSA, is pooled as "other". The groups were kept unchanged for the primary sample. The 51 primary lakes with no delineat_1 <!-- results/primary/sample_log.csv: delineat_1 missing --> are kept in the correlations and left out of the source comparison, which uses 3,223 lakes <!-- results/primary/sample_log.csv: source comparison -->. Dimension is compared across the five groups <!-- results/primary/kruskal_wallis.csv: n_groups --> with a Kruskal–Wallis test in the primary window only. That window was set in the analysis code (src/primary_analysis.py), not in the protocol.

**Within-source check.** Spearman rho is recomputed within the 2004 FSA group only, 1,543 lakes <!-- results/primary/sample_log.csv: 2004 FSA only -->, in all three windows. It is reported alongside the decision rule, not as part of it.

**Pilot-excluded sensitivity analysis.** 100 pilot lakes are in the primary sample <!-- results/primary/sample_log.csv: pilot lakes in primary -->. The primary run uses all lakes. A sensitivity analysis repeats every correlation without the pilot lakes, on 3,174 lakes <!-- results/primary/sample_log.csv: primary without pilot lakes -->.

**Post-hoc check (not in the analysis plan).** After the primary results, the primary-window dimension was recomputed with every island ring removed, so that only the outer shore of each primary lake is box-counted. It was correlated with outer-shore length and with area (src/posthoc_outer_shore.py, commit 47f5fce). No decision rule is applied to it.

**Post-hoc spacing rerun (not in the analysis plan).** After review, the primary analysis was repeated on the same 3,274 lakes <!-- results/validation/spacing_0p01/sample_log.csv: primary --> with boundary points at most 0.01 of the smallest box apart instead of 0.1 <!-- results/validation/spacing_0p01/parameters.csv: densify_fraction, planned_densify_fraction --> (src/validation_spacing.py, commit f9e2f28; results/validation/spacing_0p01/). Every other setting and statistic was as planned: the three windows, the correlations and their bootstrap intervals, the decision rule, the Kruskal–Wallis test in the primary window, the within-source check and the pilot-excluded sensitivity analysis. The planned results were not replaced. The author set a stop rule for this run: stop if the whole run took more than 10,800 s, three hours <!-- results/validation/spacing_0p01/parameters.csv: max_wall_seconds -->. The planned limit of 60 s per lake <!-- results/primary/parameters.csv: max_seconds_per_lake --> was not applied.

**Two sets of lake estimates in the later post-hoc checks.** Each post-hoc check below that uses the lake estimates was run twice: on the planned estimates (results/primary/dims.csv) and on the estimates of the spacing rerun (results/validation/spacing_0p01/dims.csv). The planned estimates are the main results, and the spacing rerun is a sensitivity check.

**Post-hoc lake width (not in the analysis plan).** For each primary lake, three widths were computed (src/validation_width.py): the diameter of the largest circle inside the lake (shapely's maximum inscribed circle, to within 1 m <!-- results/validation/width_parameters.csv: inscribed_tolerance_m -->), the short side of the minimum rotated rectangle around the lake, and a narrow-shore share for the largest box of each window. For the narrow-shore share, the lake is opened with a disk of radius r, half the largest box: r is 50 m for the 100 m box and 100 m for the 200 m box <!-- results/validation/width_parameters.csv: narrow_radius_m_20_100, narrow_radius_m_10_100, narrow_radius_m_20_200 -->. The opened lake is the lake shrunk by r and grown back by r; it leaves out parts of the lake narrower than 2r, and convex corners. Narrow shore is the shoreline more than 1 m from the opened lake <!-- results/validation/width_parameters.csv: shore_gap_m -->, and the share is its length over the total shoreline length. The author chose this definition after a trial on test shapes and six randomly chosen lakes, before the width results were computed. It replaced a first definition, the shoreline within 1 m of the part of the lake outside the opened lake, which counted much of a circle's shoreline as narrow because buffering leaves thin slivers along the whole shore. Spearman rho was computed between the narrow-shore share and the estimate in each window, and between the narrow-shore share and area, each with a percentile bootstrap interval under the planned settings. One sensitivity check was set by the author before any result: the six primary rhos were recomputed without the lakes whose narrow-shore share for that window's largest box is above 0.10 <!-- results/validation/width_parameters.csv: max_narrow_share -->, and the planned decision rule was applied to the result. Because the share depends on the largest box, the subset of lakes differs between windows.

**Post-hoc spatial block bootstrap (not in the analysis plan).** Each primary lake was assigned to a square block by its centroid in EPSG:26915, for blocks of 25, 50 and 100 km <!-- results/validation/blocks_parameters.csv: block_km_1, block_km_2, block_km_3 --> (src/validation_blocks.py). The block grid starts at the origin of the coordinate system <!-- results/validation/blocks_parameters.csv: grid_origin_x_m, grid_origin_y_m -->; this was not set by the author. Whole blocks were resampled with replacement, as many blocks as the sample has, 2,000 times with seed 0 <!-- results/validation/blocks_parameters.csv: block_resamples, block_seed -->, and percentile 95% <!-- results/validation/blocks_parameters.csv: ci_level_percent --> intervals were computed for the six primary rhos, the six rhos within 2004 FSA, and each source group's median dimension in the primary window. Each statistic resamples the blocks of its own sample: the 2004 FSA rhos resample the blocks that hold 2004 FSA lakes, and each group's median resamples the blocks that hold that group's lakes. The lake-level intervals shown beside them are the planned intervals for the rhos and, for the medians, intervals with lakes resampled under the same settings.

**Post-hoc digitizing density (not in the analysis plan).** For each primary lake, the distance between consecutive vertices along every ring, as digitized and before densifying, was summarized by its median and its 90th percentile (src/validation_vertices.py). The per-lake median spacing was compared across the five source groups with a Kruskal–Wallis test, lakes with no delineat_1 excluded, and Spearman rho was computed between the per-lake median spacing and the primary-window estimate, over all lakes and within each group. For an equalized resampling, every ring was rebuilt with vertices every 25 m and, separately, every 50 m <!-- results/validation/equalized_parameters.csv: interval_m_1, interval_m_2 --> along it, starting from the ring's own start point. A ring too short to keep 4 vertices <!-- results/validation/equalized_parameters.csv: min_ring_vertices -->, not counting the closing point, was dropped, and a dropped outer ring takes its islands with it. The primary-window estimate was recomputed from the rebuilt rings with boundary points at most 0.01 of the smallest box apart, as the author set, and also at the planned 0.1 <!-- results/validation/equalized_parameters.csv: densify_fraction_1, densify_fraction_2 -->, so that each can be set beside the matching set of lake estimates. The group medians and the Kruskal–Wallis test were repeated on each. The run used 4 worker processes and the author's stop rule: stop if the whole run took more than 10,800 s <!-- results/validation/equalized_parameters.csv: workers, max_wall_seconds -->. The rebuilding also dropped rings and left some lakes invalid, so it changed the geometry as well as the vertex spacing; the clean-subset check below repeats the comparison on the lakes it left whole.

**Post-hoc width-based exclusion (not in the analysis plan).** The narrow-shore share tracks the estimate (Table 14), so the narrow-shore sensitivity check drops lakes on a near-proxy of the estimate. After the narrow-shore results and before this check's results, the author set a second exclusion that uses each lake's widest point instead (src/validation_width_exclusion.py, commit b610563). Every lake whose inscribed-circle diameter, from the width check, is below 400 m <!-- results/validation/width_exclusion_parameters.csv: min_inscribed_diameter_m -->, twice the largest box of the 20–200 m window <!-- results/validation/width_exclusion_rho.csv: window_min_m, window_max_m -->, is dropped, and the same lakes are dropped in all three windows. On the remaining lakes, the six primary rhos were recomputed with the planned percentile bootstrap, 2,000 resamples with seed 0 <!-- results/validation/width_exclusion_parameters.csv: bootstrap_resamples, bootstrap_seed -->, and the planned decision rule was applied. Spearman rho between the inscribed diameter and the estimate was computed in each window over all primary lakes, with an interval under the same settings. The cutoff is applied to the computed diameter, which can be up to 2 m short of the true one, twice the 1 m tolerance on the radius; the dropped lakes within 2 m below the cutoff were counted <!-- results/validation/width_exclusion_counts.csv: diameter_tolerance_m, n_excluded_within_tolerance; results/validation/width_exclusion_parameters.csv: inscribed_tolerance_m -->.

**Post-hoc clean-subset check of the equalized resampling (not in the analysis plan).** Added after a second review, with settings the author set before its results (src/validation_equalized_clean.py, commit 03e8013). Each primary lake was rebuilt again at 25 m and at 50 m <!-- results/validation/equalized_clean_parameters.csv: interval_m_1, interval_m_2 -->, as in the equalized resampling, and flagged for whether the rebuilding dropped any ring and whether the result is a valid polygon.
