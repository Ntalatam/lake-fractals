## 1. Shoreline length partly encodes complexity

For lakes of the same area, a longer shoreline is a more convoluted one, and box counts between 20 and 100 m <!-- results/primary/rho_primary.csv: window 20_100, window_min_m and window_max_m --> respond to the same convolution. Shoreline length and dimension are therefore not independent measures, and part of the supported positive relationship (rho 0.4099 in the primary window) <!-- results/primary/rho_primary.csv: 20_100, shoreline --> is built into the two measures. Islands add to both: shoreline_m and the box count include every island ring, and 1,135 primary lakes have at least one <!-- results/exploratory/outer_shore/post-hoc_outer_shore_counts.csv: n_with_interior_rings -->. In the post-hoc check with island rings removed, rho is 0.3646 <!-- results/exploratory/outer_shore/post-hoc_outer_shore_rho.csv: exterior_length -->, so removing islands lowers the correlation but does not remove it. No analysis holds area fixed when relating dimension to shoreline length (for example a partial correlation, or a shoreline development index), and none was pre-registered.

## 2. Finite-size effects in the 20–200 m window <!-- results/primary/rho_primary.csv: window 20_200 -->

The smallest primary lake has an equivalent diameter of 717.8 m <!-- results/feasibility/feasibility_summary.txt: min (not a csv) -->, and the largest box in the 20–200 m window is 200 m <!-- results/primary/rho_primary.csv: window 20_200, window_max_m -->. The finite-size argument for the 100-acre cutoff (Methods) was made for the 100 m box of the primary window, and the feasibility check compared lake size only with the 100 m box <!-- results/feasibility/feasibility_summary.txt (not a csv) -->, so neither shows that the 20–200 m window <!-- results/primary/rho_primary.csv: window 20_200 --> is free of finite-size bias. In the post-hoc calibration (Methods), a 100-acre ellipse with an axis ratio of 10, whose true dimension is 1, was estimated at 1.0349 on average in this window at the planned point spacing, against 0.9986 in the primary window <!-- results/validation/calibration_summary.csv: shape ellipse, target_acres 100, aspect_ratio 10, densify_fraction 0.1, mean_estimate, windows 20_200 and 20_100 -->. The only area correlation in the primary sample whose interval excludes zero is in this window: rho -0.0590 (95% CI -0.0942 to -0.0247) <!-- results/primary/rho_primary.csv: 20_200, area -->. It may reflect finite-size bias rather than shoreline geometry.

The estimates also carry bias of their own. In the primary window, 497 of the 3,274 primary lakes (15.2%) have an estimated dimension below 1, the lowest 0.9667; there are 512 (15.6%) in the 10–100 m window and 258 (7.9%) in the 20–200 m window <!-- results/validation/below_one.csv: n_below_1, n_lakes, percent_below_1, min_below_1 --> (Figures 1 and 2). The box-counting dimension of a curve cannot be below 1, so these values are estimation error. The post-hoc calibration (Methods) measured this error on shapes of known dimension. In the primary window at the planned point spacing, the mean bias for ellipses of 100 to 10,000 acres, whose true dimension is 1, ran from -0.0143 to -0.0014 <!-- results/validation/calibration_summary.csv: shape ellipse, window 20_100, densify_fraction 0.1, bias -->, and the bias for the circle was -0.0137 <!-- results/validation/calibration_summary.csv: shape circle, window 20_100, densify_fraction 0.1, bias -->. Generalized Koch snowflakes of dimension 1.02, 1.05 and 1.10 were estimated at 1.0155, 1.0461 and 1.1099 <!-- results/validation/calibration_summary.csv: shape koch, window 20_100, densify_fraction 0.1, known_d, mean_estimate -->. With boundary points at most 0.01 of the smallest box apart, the mean ellipse bias in the primary window ran from -0.0015 to 0.0089 <!-- results/validation/calibration_summary.csv: shape ellipse, window 20_100, densify_fraction 0.01, bias -->. In the primary analysis rerun at that spacing (Methods), 138 lakes (4.2%) are below 1 in the primary window, 110 (3.4%) in the 10–100 m window and 74 (2.3%) in the 20–200 m window <!-- results/validation/spacing_0p01/below_one.csv: n_below_1, percent_below_1 -->. The source-group medians in the primary window are 1.0198 to 1.0529, against 1.0100 to 1.0444 at the planned spacing <!-- results/validation/spacing_0p01/comparison_source_groups.csv: median_dim_rerun, median_dim_planned -->. The rerun changed no decision-rule outcome, no sign of rho and no interval's inclusion of zero <!-- results/validation/spacing_0p01/comparison_decision_rule.csv: same_outcome; results/validation/spacing_0p01/comparison_rho.csv: same_sign, same_ci_status -->. The calibration used synthetic shapes, not lake shorelines, so it does not give the error of any lake's estimate or show whether that error depends on lake size.

## 3. Source differences are smaller than the test tolerance

The median dimensions of the source groups run from 1.0100 (DNR 24k POLYS) to 1.0444 (PWI) <!-- results/primary/source_groups.csv: median_dim -->. The tests of the estimator check only that estimates fall within 0.05 of known dimensions <!-- tests/test_boxcount.py:28 (TOLERANCE), not in results/ -->, a margin wider than the whole spread of group medians. The post-hoc calibration measured the bias near 1 more finely (Section 2), but on synthetic shapes, not on shorelines from each source. The Kruskal–Wallis test (H = 170.90) <!-- results/primary/kruskal_wallis.csv: H --> shows that dimension differs between the groups; at the finer point spacing of the post-hoc rerun, H is 164.98 <!-- results/validation/spacing_0p01/kruskal_wallis.csv: H -->. Neither check can separate a mapping effect from estimator bias that differs between groups. Mapping source was also not assigned at random: the groups may differ in lake size, region and digitizing detail, and the comparison controls for none of these.

## 4. The 1,000 m shoreline filter selects on shape <!-- results/filter_log.csv: step 4, description -->

At 10 acres, a circular lake has a shoreline shorter than 1,000 m <!-- 10: results/filter_log.csv, step 2 description; 1,000 m: step 4 description; that the circle's shoreline is shorter is a geometric fact, not in results/ -->. Step 4 therefore removes small lakes with compact shorelines and keeps a small lake only if its shoreline is long for its area. It removed 1,258 lakes <!-- results/filter_log.csv: step 4, dropped -->. Among the smallest lakes in the exploratory sample, this favors convoluted shorelines, which may raise their dimensions and push the exploratory area correlation (rho -0.2831) <!-- results/primary/exploratory_10acre_rho.csv: area --> toward negative values. The size of this effect was not measured. The filter removes no lake from the primary sample <!-- CLAUDE.md:17, not in results/ -->.

## 5. Lakes are not independent

Lakes cluster in space (Figure 4), share regional geology, and were mapped in regional projects, so neighboring lakes are likely to have similar shorelines and the same mapping source. The p-values and the bootstrap intervals treat lakes as independent (the bootstrap resamples single lakes), so both are likely too narrow. No spatial structure was modeled.

## 6. No multiple-comparison correction

The primary sample alone gives six correlations, two measures in three windows <!-- results/primary/rho_primary.csv: one row per measure and window -->. The within-source, pilot-excluded, exploratory and post-hoc checks, the post-hoc spacing rerun and the Kruskal–Wallis test add more. Every p-value is unadjusted. The decision rule uses only the sign of rho, so it is unaffected, but any statement of significance should allow for the number of tests.

## 7. The 20 m lower bound assumes map-based digitizing <!-- results/primary/rho_primary.csv: window 20_100, window_min_m -->

Of the 3,274 primary lakes <!-- results/primary/sample_log.csv: primary -->, 1,543 (the 2004 FSA group) <!-- results/primary/source_groups.csv: 2004 FSA, n --> were digitized from 2004 aerial imagery, not 1:24,000 maps <!-- the year is in the delineat_1 value "2004 FSA"; that these shorelines were digitized from aerial imagery is the author's statement, best cited to the DNR layer metadata; 1:24,000 is the map scale in Methods (US National Map Accuracy Standards); not in results/ -->. The rationale for the primary window's 20 m lower bound <!-- results/primary/rho_primary.csv: window 20_100, window_min_m --> rests on the accuracy of digitizing from those maps (Methods), so it fits these lakes less exactly.
